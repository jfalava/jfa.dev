title: Identity
description: How KEWEKE creates and uses an Ed25519 identity to sign your edits.
---

KEWEKE identifies you with cryptography, not with a password or email. The goal is simple: your browser can prove it is you, even offline, and other browsers can recognize you if you link them.

## How your identity is made

On your first visit your browser generates two Ed25519 keypairs:

- a **user key** — represents you across devices
- a **device key** — represents this browser specifically

The public half of each key is hashed (SHA-256) to make your `userId` and `deviceId`. Those short fingerprints are what the service stores — never the private key. Private keys stay inside IndexedDB in your browser and are never sent over the network.

You then pick a display name. That name is the only thing other people see. Changing it updates the name attached to your future edits.

> Implementation lives in the [auth identity component](https://github.com/jfalava/jfa.dev/tree/main/web/keweke/src/features/auth/lib) and the shared schemas in [common identities](https://github.com/jfalava/jfa.dev/tree/main/common/src/identities.ts).

## The three states

- **Anonymous** — no name yet. You can create lists and add items, but badges show as “unsigned.”
- **Local** — you chose a name, but have not linked it to the service. Your lists work offline and your name appears locally.
- **Remote** — your name is confirmed by the service and linked to your key. You can now publish lists, edit shared lists, and approve other browsers.

Moving from local to remote happens when you ask the service to create or adopt a remote user. Your browser signs the request with your device key and the service verifies the signature.

## Pairing another browser

There are two ways to bring the same identity to a new device:

- **Passkey** — if your remote user already has a passkey, the new browser can adopt it directly.
- **Pairing code** — your new browser shows a ten-character code, an already-approved browser approves it, and the new browser is linked.

After pairing the new browser holds the same `userId` and your display name, and its avatar — built from your `userId` plus your name — will match the other devices.

## Avatars

Avatars are deterministic shapes made from your `userId` and your current name. Rename yourself and the shape changes, but the identity behind it stays the same.

> See the [user settings component](https://github.com/jfalava/jfa.dev/tree/main/web/keweke/src/features/auth/components) for how the avatar and the “Anonymous / Local / Remote” badge are rendered.
